Understanding a Research Article: Section-by-Section Analysis 
Purpose of this Exercise 
Many students read research papers without understanding why each section exists. A 
research paper is like a story: 
• The Introduction explains the problem. 
• The Literature Review explains what others have done. 
• The Methodology explains how the work was carried out. 
• The Results explain what was discovered. 
• The Discussion explains why the results matter. 
• The Conclusion explains the contribution and future direction. 
As a researcher or reviewer, you should be able to evaluate each section independently. 
 
Section 1: Introduction 
Why is this Section Important? 
The Introduction convinces the reader that the problem is important and worth solving. 
Questions a Reviewer Asks 
1. Is the problem clearly explained? 
2. Why is the problem important? 
3. What are the consequences if the problem is not solved? 
4. Does the author provide sufficient background? 
5. Is the research objective clearly stated? 
Example 
Statement: 
"Improper waste segregation increases recycling costs and environmental pollution." 
Reviewer Thinking: 
✓ Is this problem important? 
✓ Is there evidence supporting this statement? 
✓ Does the reader understand the motivation? 
Common Mistakes 
• Problem is unclear.

Section 2: Literature Review 
Why is this Section Important? 
The Literature Review shows what previous researchers have already done. 
Without a literature review, the reviewer cannot determine whether the work is new. 
Questions a Reviewer Asks 
1. What solutions already exist? 
2. What are their limitations? 
3. What gap remains unsolved? 
4. Is the proposed work different? 
Example 
Previous Studies: 
• Study A: Manual waste segregation. 
• Study B: Smart bins with fill-level monitoring. 
Research Gap: 
"No study performs automatic waste classification using AI at the point of disposal." 
Common Mistakes 
• Listing papers without analysis.

• No comparison among studies. 
• No research gap. 
• No justification for new work.

Section 3: Methodology 
Why is this Section Important? 
The methodology explains exactly how the work was performed. 
A good methodology allows another researcher to repeat the experiment. 
Questions a Reviewer Asks 
1. Can the experiment be reproduced? 
2. Are tools and equipment described? 
3. Is the sample size sufficient? 
4. Are assumptions clearly stated? 
5. Is the methodology scientifically valid? 
Example 
The authors used: 
• Raspberry Pi 
• Camera Module 
• CNN Algorithm 
• 3000 Images 
Reviewer Thinking: 
✓ How were images collected? 

=== PAGE 4 ===
✓ Was the dataset balanced? 
✓ Which CNN architecture was used? 
Common Mistakes 
• Missing experimental details. 
• No explanation of parameters. 
• Small sample size. 
• Unclear procedure.

Section 4: Results 
Why is this Section Important? 
Results show whether the proposed method actually works. 
Questions a Reviewer Asks 
1. Are results presented clearly? 
2. Are results supported by data? 
3. Are comparisons provided? 
4. Are the improvements significant? 
Example 
Accuracy = 92% 
Reviewer Thinking: 
✓ Is 92% good? 

=== PAGE 5 ===
✓ Better than what? 
✓ Is the improvement statistically significant? 
Common Mistakes 
• Only reporting numbers. 
• No baseline comparison. 
• No error analysis. 
• Insufficient experiments.

Section 5: Discussion 
Why is this Section Important? 
Results tell what happened. 
Discussion explains why it happened. 
Questions a Reviewer Asks 
1. Why did the method perform well? 
2. Why did errors occur? 
3. What are the practical implications? 
4. What are the limitations? 
Example 
"The system misclassified damaged waste items."

Section 6: Conclusion 
Why is this Section Important? 
The conclusion summarizes the contribution of the work. 
Questions a Reviewer Asks 
1. Did the authors achieve their objective? 
2. What is the key contribution? 
3. Is the conclusion supported by the results? 
4. Is future work suggested? 
Common Mistakes
